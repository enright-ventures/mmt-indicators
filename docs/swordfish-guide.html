Swordfish Indicator Guide

What each indicator on the Swordfish chart draws, how to read it, which settings matter, and how far each one has been checked against outside data.

Written 15 Oct 2026. Covers the six Swordfish scripts and MMT's built-in Open Interest and Delta Volume panes.

The Swordfish chart

One 30m chart of BTC/USD on Binance perps. A single chart matters: in a two-chart layout, scripts that wait for the live bar stalled.

Price paneComposites (Daily and Weekly), Calendar VWAP (weekly focus with monthly, quarterly and yearly levels), Rolling VWAP (7D, 30D, 365D), the Delta Heat ladder on every candle, and Offside (trap markers, divergences, reaction zones, flow-weighted price proxies).
Open InterestMMT built-in, Binance, Bybit and OKX perps combined, as candles.
Delta VolumeMMT built-in, the same three perps combined, buy minus sell volume per bar.
Bar StatsSwordfish table, one column per bar: Long liqs, Short liqs, +L proxy, +S proxy, Volume.

Reading for offside traders

A suggested order for reading failed breaks and the surrounding flow.

  • Where is price against the big references? Composite value areas, the calendar VWAPs and their ±1 SD, and the rolling VWAPs show where the market has accepted price. Traders who entered far from these are the ones most likely to be offside.
  • Where was flow concentrated? The dotted +L and +S proxy lines show flow-weighted prices, not participant entries, inventory or P&L.
  • Did a level just trap someone? A blue triangle under a bar marks a failed breakdown that held (shorts trapped); a red one above marks a failed breakout (longs trapped). The corner panel names the level, the signed flow proxy during the break, and its VWAP.
  • Did flow reverse? Counterflow is an absolute signed-flow proxy after confirmation. It cannot measure remaining trapped positions.
  • Is it forced? The Long liqs and Short liqs rows in Bar Stats show forced exits, bar by bar.

Swordfish Composites v2

Value areas of balanced periods, merged when they overlap. On the chart twice: Daily composites and Weekly composites.

Draws
A box for each composite's value area (70% of its time), a line at its point of control (POC), dates inside the box, and the most recent composites' VAH, POC and VAL extended right with price labels at the edge. The value area shading is strongest on the newest composite.
Method
Time-at-price profiles from closed 30m bars, one row per 0.05% of price. Consecutive UTC days (or weeks) merge into one composite when their value areas overlap by at least 50%. The rules match the original OpenMarket kScript row for row: pairs, then extensions, then longer aggregates.
Read it
A composite is a range the market accepted. Inside it, expect rotation; away from it, traders who built positions there are either comfortable or offside. Extended levels act as reference prices.
Settings
Composite period (Daily, Weekly, Monthly), display and calculation windows (default 30 days or 13 weeks), recent composites with rays (4), value-area shading (16%), and the oldest composite drawn at 25% of the newest's strength: boxes, distributions, levels and rays fade with age so recent composites stand out (100 turns the fade off). On this chart: one copy Daily, one Weekly.
Checked
Identical to the Python reference (composites_ref.py) on BTC: 7 daily and 4 weekly composites.

Swordfish Calendar VWAP

VWAPs anchored to calendar periods, with ±1σ (one standard deviation) bands.

Draws
The focused period's developing VWAP with a ±1 SD band (weekly on this chart). The current and previous week, month, quarter and year as levels, each with ±1σ bands, labelled +1SD and -1SD on the chart. Older quarters keep their bands with labels such as 2026 Q1 +1SD. These are standard-deviation bands around the VWAP, not value areas (Composites draw the TPO value areas). A levels window (badge beside the name) lists every period.
Method
HLC3 × volume, Binance and Bybit perps pooled into one weighted mean and variance. Week and month use 30m candles; quarter and year use 4h. A period is withheld unless every pooled exchange's data starts at the period start with no gaps. Both exchanges reach 2022 on MMT; OKX (from Jan 2026) is left out so older periods stay complete.
Read it
The VWAP is the average price paid in the period. Price above a period's VWAP means its average buyer is in profit and its average seller is not. The ±1σ lines mark one standard deviation of the period's prices around its VWAP: outside them, price is stretched relative to the period's average. The developing period is drawn from the forming candle's period, so a new week starts at Monday 00:00 UTC, and it is withheld if a venue's forming candle is missing, stale, or not the current candle (checked against a one-minute clock, so a feed that stops advancing is caught).
Settings
Panel anchor (Weekly here), which units to show, +/-1 SD bands and labels on older periods (All here), completed periods kept, venues pooled.
Checked
Binance only: within 0.01% of Binance's own data, including the 2024 yearly VWAP (3,063.02 on ETH, where OpenMarket's data had been wrong). Pooled: this week's VWAP 82,736.90 vs 82,736.97 from Binance and Bybit's own candles, ±1 SD within 7 cents.

Swordfish Rolling VWAP

The forming candle is included within each window: 335/1,439 closed 30m candles plus the forming candle, or 364 closed daily candles plus the forming day. A window shows only when its candles are consecutive on every pooled venue, so a missing candle can never stretch "7D" over more than seven days; after a gap in the exchange data the line returns once a full gap-free window has built up. On the live bar each venue's forming candle must follow its last closed one and be the current candle (checked against a one-minute clock and the chart's forming bar), and the venues must end on the same candle.

7-day, 30-day and 365-day candle-aligned VWAPs, including the forming candle within the stated candle count.

Draws
7D and 30D as dashed lines, 365D as a thinner solid white line, each named at the right edge.
Method
HLC3 × volume, Binance and Bybit perps pooled: each exchange keeps its own trailing window and the VWAP is their summed price × volume over summed volume. 7D and 30D roll over 336 and 1,440 bars of 30m candles; 365D rolls over daily candles and steps once a day. The candle still forming is included.
Read it
The average cost of everyone active over that window. Price below the 30D line means the past month's average buyer is underwater.
Checked
Pooled, against Binance and Bybit's own candles: 7D 82,583.20 vs 82,583.29; 30D 82,809.70 vs 82,809.65; 365D 77,716 vs 77,711.

Swordfish Delta Heat

Live reconstruction supports whole-minute chart timeframes from 1m through 4h and can lag by one minute. Other timeframes show closed bars only; the title identifies this limit.

A delta ladder on every candle: who was aggressive at each price inside the bar, in dollars.

RowsEach price row is a lane. The row size is automatic: a tenth of the average range of the last 100 bars, on a 1/2/2.5/5 grid ($50 on BTC 30m). It changes only when that range doubles or halves, and then every candle is redrawn at the new size, so all ladders on screen share one row height. The bar's length is the row's net delta against the candle's largest row: blue for buying, red for selling. The number is the dollar value. The candle's largest row is outlined.
Candle stripThe chart's candle runs as a thin strip at the bar's left edge, so the ladder can use the bar's width.
Scale
Length is relative to the candle's own largest row, which runs full length and is outlined. Colour strength compares across candles: full colour at 2× a typical bar's largest row (the average over the last 200 bars), so a quiet candle's biggest row is full length but pale.
Data
MMT's per-bar volume snapshots, summed over Binance, Bybit and OKX perps. These cover about the last 1,000 bars (21 days on 30m). The live bar is rebuilt from completed one-minute snapshots, so it is at most a minute behind.
Read it
Heavy buying at the top of a bar that closed lower means buyers paid up and got rejected. Offside turns that into a marker.
Live bar
Rebuilt from MMT's completed one-minute snapshots since the bar opened. A missing minute (no trades, or no data) is skipped rather than ending the rebuild, so earlier minutes are kept, and the title shows the shortfall, e.g. "live ladder: 28 of 30 minutes". The closed bar's own snapshot replaces the rebuild when the bar closes.

Swordfish Offside

Where traders are caught offside, following Stable workshop 11: a level that matters breaks, price reclaims it, and the reclaim holds. Each break is measured from MMT's 5m volume profiles, with break-flow and counterflow proxies beside it.

Key

Trapped shortsBlue triangle under the flush low: a failed breakdown. Price broke under the level (thin line), closed back above it within the hour, and held for 15 minutes. Placed on the bar where the hold completed.
Trapped longsRed triangle above the flush high: a failed breakout, the mirror image.
Marker sizeBigger and solid when the size pressed through the level (from the volume profile) beats its recent average.
Level and average fillThe solid line is the broken level. The dotted line is the volume-weighted average price of the pressing beyond the level ("sellers' avg fill below level"). Without a volume profile it falls back to the break bars' volume-weighted HLC3 ("break VWAP").
Delta divergenceDashed line joining two highs (red) or lows (blue): price made a new 20-bar extreme but cumulative delta did not.
Reaction zoneA price row with outsized delta (2.5× a typical bar's largest row). Red for selling (resistance), blue for buying (support). Extends right until a bar closes through it, then fades.
Flow-weighted pricesGreen: +L proxy; red: +S proxy. These are price/flow summaries, not open positions.

How a trap is found

Workshop 11's three questions, made measurable. Judged on 5m bars whatever the chart's timeframe:

  • A level that matters. By default, 4h swing highs and lows (the middle of 7 bars, below the 3 before and not above the 3 after), watched for 14 days. Swing lows can only fail as breakdowns, swing highs as breakouts.
  • The break. A 5m bar trades through the level by at least 0.03% of price, coming from the other side.
  • The reclaim. A 5m close back on the original side within 60 minutes. A break that stays through the level longer than that counts as acceptance, and the level is dropped.
  • The hold (the trigger). The next three 5m closes (15 minutes) stay on the reclaimed side and the flush extreme is not taken. If a close slips back through within the hour, the break continues; if the flush extreme is taken, there is no trap.
  • Measured from 5m volume profiles. MMT's buy and sell volume per price for each 5m bar, combined across Binance, Bybit and OKX perps by MMT. Every native profile price (about $2.50 apart on BTC) is classified against the level by its own price, including volume another venue traded outside the chart venue's candle; volume at the level's own price counts on neither side. No individual trades are loaded; MMT keeps about 16 days of 5m profiles, so traps days old are measured and the script reaches the live bar in seconds:
    • Pressed: aggressive selling below a broken support on broken bars through the reclaim close (buying above a broken resistance for failed breakouts); holding bars do not add pressing.
    • Avg fill: the volume-weighted price of that pressing, at each profile level's own price (so never on the wrong side of the level); the profile's price resolution still bounds how exactly a trade's price is known.
    • Reclaim x: the opposite aggression back on the reclaimed side, from the successful reclaim through the hold, divided by the pressed size. A failed reclaim resets this back-volume before another attempt. Workshop 11's reclaim on volume: above 1, more came back than pressed.
    A bar without its profile makes the trap's readings unavailable: it shows "no volume profile" and the break VWAP instead.
  • Flow proxies from candles and OI. +S = (dOI − delta) / 2 and +L = (dOI + delta) / 2 cannot identify positions or participants. "Break flow proxy" sums the positive proxy over the break, including the reclaim bar. Liquidations of the trapped side are reported separately.
  • Stop and invalidation. The flush extreme. A trap also fails if a 5m close goes back through the level.
  • One trap per side per hour, since nearby levels often break together.

Optional levels (settings, off by default): weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly VWAP and ±1 SD, the prior period's, older quarters', and the developing period's (taken at each UTC day start). These come from the chart market's 4h candles, so they can sit slightly off the Calendar VWAP's lines, which pool Binance and Bybit and use 30m candles for week and month. Pooling here made the script too slow to load. VWAP levels can trap either way; the side is set by where price breaks from.

Old wick markers (setting, off by default): the earlier method, heavy net delta inside a rejected wick. Kept for comparison; the backtest found no edge in it.

Readouts

Corner panel (bottom right of the price pane): the newest trap on each side. For example:

Illustrative format: Trapped shorts [UTC time] | 4h swing low [level] | pressed [size] below at avg [fill] | reclaim [ratio]x | [size] longs liq | stop [price] | counterflow proxy [size] | holding
  • Time is the 5m bar where the hold completed, in UTC (the chart shows local time).
  • Status:
    • "holding" while tracked;
    • "failed: closed back below (above) the level" or "failed: flush low (high) taken" when the trap fails;
    • "tracked 24h, ended" after the tracking time.

Recent traps window (badge beside the script's name): the last 40 traps, with level, pressed, avg fill, reclaim x, longs or shorts liquidated, stop, break-flow proxy, counterflow proxy, measured net flow since the hold, and status. Columns sort.

Right edge: +L and +S flow-weighted prices, proxy weights and price distance. These are not position sizes, entries or P&L.

Counterflow proxy

After the hold, the opposite sign of the same proxy (−S for trapped shorts, −L for trapped longs) accumulates in coins. It does not identify who closed a position, and it never ends price tracking, even when it exceeds the break-flow proxy or that proxy is zero. The measured net flow (buys minus sells from candles) since the hold sits beside it in the Recent traps window. Only price invalidation or the time limit ends tracking.

Flow-weighted price proxies

The signed formulas are NL = (dOI + D) / 2 and NS = (dOI - D) / 2. Positive values add weight at HLC3, negative values reduce weight, and weights fade using the half-life in hours. These assumptions do not reconstruct participant holdings.

All selected venues need aligned candles and OI. Missing break data withholds the break-flow total; missing subsequent data withholds counterflow. Liquidations count only when MMT's liquidation stat is on the same bar, otherwise the trap shows "liquidations n/a". The flow-weighted prices reset to unavailable after a missing chart bar or a bar without aligned candles and OI, and rebuild from valid bars. A missing 5m price bar drops any break or hold in progress, ends tracked traps ("ended: 5m data gap"), and no break is read on the first bar after it; the 60-minute reclaim and the three-bar hold are counted on consecutive 5m bars.

Swordfish Bar Stats

A table under the chart, one column per bar, shaded by sign and by size against that row's last 48 bars.

Volume rowGreen from the left is the buy share, red from the right the sell share. The number is the net delta (buy − sell). Stronger colour means more total volume.
RowMeaningSource
Long liqsLongs forcibly closed on the bar (forced selling, red)Binance, Bybit, OKX perps
Short liqsShorts forcibly closed (forced buying, green)Same three venues
+L proxySigned +L flow proxy: (dOI + D) / 2Same three venues
+S proxySigned +S flow proxy: (dOI − D) / 2Same three venues
VolumeBuy and sell split, net delta as the numberSame three venues

A column is shown only when every selected venue's candle and the open interest are on that bar (liquidation cells are checked separately); otherwise the cells stay empty rather than summing a venue that is still on the previous bar. The shading scale learns only from complete bars.

Open interest and liquidations come from MMT's combined streams. MMT gives scripts no combined candles, so buy and sell volume is the three venues' candles summed. That sum matches MMT's combined volume delta to within 0.03 BTC per bar. The "Data from" setting switches to Binance + Bybit or the chart's venue.

Values are in BTC. The sum of the two proxies equals the change in open interest, and their difference equals the delta. MMT names long liquidations buyLiq. That was checked on 3,000 one-minute bars: minutes with buyLiq averaged −15 bp and minutes with sellLiq +15 bp.

Open Interest and Delta Volume

MMT's built-in panes, added as chart layers.

  • Open Interest: Binance, Bybit and OKX perps combined (MMT's "Aggregate" setting, 3 markets). It shows open interest as candles, so you can see whether positions are being added (rising) or closed (falling) during a move.
  • Delta Volume: the same three perps combined. It shows each bar's buy minus sell volume.

They are MMT's own panes; their data source is set in each pane's settings (gear beside the name) under Aggregate → Markets.

Importing a TradingView watchlist

Export the list in TradingView ("Export list"), then run the importer. With no file given it opens a file picker in Downloads. It converts each symbol to the MMT market (BINANCE:TIAUSDT.P → Binance perps tia/usd, COINBASE:BTCUSD → Coinbase btc/usd), clears the watchlist in your open MMT session and adds the new markets in file order. MMT watchlists have no sections, so ### headers only appear in the importer's report. Add --dry-run to see the conversion without changing anything.

python3 tools/tv_watchlist_import.py python3 tools/tv_watchlist_import.py "~/Downloads/vyaps - TradingView Watchlist.txt" --dry-run
